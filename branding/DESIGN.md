# Equal Weave

*The visual philosophy behind the Meshboard mark.*

**No one on top.** Three sheets of paper form a closed ring. Each rests on one neighbour and slips
beneath the other, so the order of the stack is a loop rather than a hierarchy. It is the quiet
geometry of a peer-to-peer network: every participant carries the others and is carried in turn,
and there is no server sitting above the group.

**Geometry without noise.** Three identical squares, one corner radius, one gap width, placed on a
perfect circle with equal rotation. There are no gradients, no outlines and no shadows: depth comes
only from the order of the sheets and the thin channel of ink where one passes over another. Every
measure derives from a single system and every margin matches its opposite. The mark should feel
like the result of countless patient refinements, the work of someone who will not accept a
misplaced tenth of a point.

**Colour as language.** A restrained palette borrowed from sticky-note paper but made more
grown-up: a warm yellow, a coral and a clear sky blue, set on a deep ink ground that lets the paper
glow without competing with it. The three hues are calibrated so none dominates and the mark stays
bright and legible when reduced to sixteen pixels.

**Scale and silence.** The mark fills about two thirds of its tile and lets the edges breathe. It
must hold its character from favicon to 1024 pixels: small, you read three colours meeting in a
ring; large, you discover the precision of each fold. Nothing disappears when it shrinks, and
nothing exists only to fill space.

**Words at a minimum.** The name appears only where it is needed, in a sober sans-serif, never
inside the symbol. The form says what matters, with the meticulous care of someone who wants the
mark to feel inevitable, as if it could not have been drawn any other way.
